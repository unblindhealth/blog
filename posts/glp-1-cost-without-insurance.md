If you are paying for a GLP-1 yourself, **do not start with the list price**. In 2026, manufacturer self-pay programs can put the price well below list price — but the best route depends on your drug, dose and insurance.

If you have Medicare, check coverage **before** paying cash: eligible Part D beneficiaries can now get certain weight-management GLP-1s for **$50 a month** through the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge.

For everyone else, compare your insurance price, manufacturer program and pharmacy cash price.

## What is the cheapest order to check?

**Start with the payment path that could beat cash.**

1. **Medicare Part D:** Check whether your prescription belongs under regular Part D or the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge before paying cash.
2. **Medicaid:** Check your state program and plan. Coverage for weight-management GLP-1s varies, and the federal BALANCE model is voluntary for states.
3. **Commercial insurance:** Ask for your exact patient responsibility, then check whether a manufacturer savings card lowers it.
4. **No useful insurance coverage:** Compare the manufacturer self-pay price with the pharmacy cash price.

That order matters because paying outside insurance can mean the purchase does not count toward your deductible or out-of-pocket maximum.

## Why can the advertised price be misleading?

Because **"starting at $149" or "$199 for Wegovy" is not one permanent price for every patient and dose**.

Foundayo pricing changes by dose. Wegovy and Ozempic have limited-time starter offers for eligible new patients. Zepbound's higher-dose promotional pricing can depend on refilling within a stated window.

The useful question is not:

**What is the advertised GLP-1 price?**

It is:

**What will my exact prescription cost this month, and what does that payment do to my insurance benefits?**

## What if insurance says the drug is covered?

"Covered" still does not tell you what you will pay.

Ask your insurer or pharmacy for your **patient responsibility** after any deductible, copay or coinsurance. Then compare that amount with the manufacturer price.

Suppose your insurance price is $430 and a self-pay option is $349. Cash saves $81 today. But if the $430 would count toward your deductible and the $349 would not, you have a second tradeoff to consider.

For more on that decision, see [GLP-1 savings cards vs self-pay](/blog/glp-1-savings-card-vs-self-pay).

## What should I ask before I fill the prescription?

You can usually get what you need with four questions:

- **What will I pay if you run this through my insurance?**
- **Is there a manufacturer savings program for my exact prescription?**
- **What is the manufacturer self-pay price for this dose?**
- **If I pay outside insurance, will it count toward my deductible or out-of-pocket maximum?**

If you have Medicare, use the [2026 GLP-1 insurance guide](/blog/glp-1-insurance-coverage) before paying cash. The new federal rules can produce a very different price.

## Bottom line

There is no single "GLP-1 cash price."

In 2026, the lowest legitimate price may come from your insurance, a manufacturer savings card, a manufacturer self-pay program or — for eligible Medicare patients — the $50 Medicare GLP-1 Bridge.
