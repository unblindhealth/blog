The Walmart $4 prescription list is one of the simpler prescription-pricing programs to check. But the name can be misleading.

Walmart still offers a low-cost prescription program in 2026. Its current pharmacy page advertises **select generic drugs at commonly prescribed dosages starting at $4 for up to a 30-day supply and $10 for a 90-day supply**. Walmart also says prices can vary by state and that other restrictions may apply.

The important word is **starting**.

Not every medication on Walmart's current list costs $4. Some are priced at $9, $15, $24 or more depending on the drug, dose, quantity and supply length.

So if you're trying to answer one question — *How much will my prescription cost at Walmart?* — don't stop after seeing your drug name on an old “$4 list.” Check the current drug, strength, quantity and price tier.

## Is the Walmart $4 prescription list still available in 2026?

Yes. Walmart's current [low-cost Prescription Program page](https://www.walmart.com/cp/4-prescriptions/1078664) continues to advertise prescriptions starting at $4 for a 30-day supply and $10 for a 90-day supply.

Walmart also referred to the $4 generic prescription program as active in a [September 2025 pharmacy announcement](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2025/09/22/same-day-pharmacy-delivery-just-got-cooler-walmart-now-delivers-refrigerated-and-reconstituted-prescriptions-nationwide).

The [current U.S./Puerto Rico retail drug list linked by Walmart](https://i5.walmartimages.com/dfw/4ff9c6c9-119c/k2-_c3e2f451-7a63-414e-aac2-e3defc13ef60.v1.pdf) is marked effective March 24, 2025. Walmart's program details say the list can change and can vary by state, so the pharmacy's current list is more useful than an older article or screenshot saved somewhere online.

## The first thing to know: it isn't really a flat $4 list

The program is commonly called the “Walmart $4 list,” but Walmart's current published list has multiple price tiers.

For example, the current list includes:

- Some medications at **$4 for up to a 30-day supply and $10 for a 90-day supply**
- Some medications at **$9 for up to a 30-day supply and $24 for a 90-day supply**
- Some medications at **$15 for a 30-day supply and $38 for a 90-day supply**
- Certain prepackaged medications with their own listed price

That distinction matters because a drug can be part of Walmart's low-cost program without being a $4 prescription.

For example, **metformin** appears on Walmart's current published list, but the listed price tier is $9 for the specified 30-day quantity and $24 for the specified 90-day quantity — not $4 and $10.

If you remember a medication being $4 years ago, check again before assuming the price is unchanged.

## Four details that can change what you pay

### 1. The dose and formulation matter

Finding the medication name isn't enough.

Walmart states that its program applies to certain generic drugs at commonly prescribed dosages and that **higher dosages can cost more**. It also says not every formulation is covered. An extended-release version, for example, may be treated differently from an immediate-release version.

Check the exact strength and formulation written on your prescription.

### 2. The quantity matters

The program is based on specified quantities, not simply “one prescription.”

A 30-day supply and 90-day supply may have different listed quantities depending on how the medication is normally taken. Prepackaged medications are covered only in the unit sizes Walmart specifies.

So two prescriptions for the same drug and strength can still price differently if the quantities don't match the program list.

### 3. Prices can vary by state

Walmart says prices for some drugs may be higher or vary in certain states, including but not necessarily limited to California and Minnesota.

Its program details also say the retail drug list itself may vary by state.

That means the national list is a starting point, not a guaranteed checkout price everywhere.

### 4. The list can change

Walmart's program details say the retail drug list may change and that program details are subject to change without advance notice.

If this is a medication you refill regularly, it makes sense to recheck the current price occasionally rather than assuming last year's price still applies.

## Do you need insurance to use the Walmart $4 prescription program?

Walmart's published program is a retail pricing program for covered prescriptions. Its official program details also say you may pay **less or more than the program price depending on your health plan**.

The practical takeaway: if you have insurance, compare the Walmart program price with the amount you'd pay using your plan.

A lower cash price can be useful, but don't assume paying outside insurance works the same as an insurance claim. If deductible or out-of-pocket tracking matters to you, ask your insurer how an outside-of-plan purchase would be treated before deciding how to pay.

## Do you need a Walmart+ membership?

Walmart's $4 prescription program is separate from Walmart+ prescription benefits.

You don't need to buy a Walmart+ membership simply to access Walmart's published low-cost retail prescription program. Walmart+ may have separate pharmacy or delivery benefits, but those are not the same thing as the $4 Prescription Program.

## What about 90-day prescriptions?

For medications in the $4/$10 tier, $10 for a 90-day supply can cost less than paying $4 three separate times.

But the 90-day option is not automatic. Walmart notes that prescriber permission may be required to change a 30-day prescription to a 90-day prescription, and some health plans may not cover 90-day supplies.

Whether a 90-day supply is medically appropriate is something to discuss with your prescriber or pharmacist. The pricing program doesn't change that.

## Are antibiotics on the Walmart $4 list?

Older articles and lists can create confusion here.

I did **not** find antibiotics listed on the current U.S./Puerto Rico retail drug list that Walmart links from its Prescription Program page. Rather than relying on an older list, check the current Walmart list or ask your local Walmart Pharmacy about the exact antibiotic, strength and quantity you've been prescribed.

This is a good example of why current source material matters. Prescription lists change.

## Is the Walmart $4 list always cheaper than insurance or a coupon?

No. There isn't one payment method that's always cheapest.

Walmart itself says you may pay less or more than its program price depending on your health plan. A prescription discount service or another pharmacy's cash price may also be lower for a particular drug.

The useful comparison is not “insurance versus Walmart” in the abstract. It's the actual price for **your exact prescription**.

Before your next refill, compare:

1. Your insurance copay or coinsurance
2. Walmart's current program price for the exact drug, strength and quantity
3. Other available cash or discount prices

Then decide which payment option makes sense for that fill.

## The one thing to do before your next refill

Look up the exact medication on Walmart's [current low-cost prescription page](https://www.walmart.com/cp/4-prescriptions/1078664), open the current drug list, and match all four details:

- Drug name
- Formulation
- Strength
- Quantity

Then confirm the price with your local Walmart Pharmacy, especially if you live in a state where pricing may differ.

It's a small check. But it's more reliable than assuming a medication is $4 because you saw it on a list years ago.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the Walmart $4 prescription list still available in 2026?

Yes. Walmart continues to publish its low-cost Prescription Program, with select generic medications starting at $4 for up to a 30-day supply and $10 for a 90-day supply. The specific list and pricing can change.
