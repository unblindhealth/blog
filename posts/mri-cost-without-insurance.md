An MRI without insurance can cost a few hundred dollars or several thousand. The scan ordered, facility, price type, and included services all affect the number.

Here is how wide the gap can be. A 2026 [analysis of cash prices published by 274 U.S. hospitals](https://medicalpricecheck.com/research/mri-cost/) found that a brain MRI without contrast, CPT code 70551, ranged from **$406 to $14,681**. The median posted cash price in the sample was $2,207.

That is a 36x spread for the same billing code.

This is not a national guarantee. The analysis excluded independent centers, and some prices may exclude other charges. The takeaway: **where you book can change the price dramatically.**

## How much does an MRI cost without insurance?

There is no single national cash price for an MRI. The most useful number is the written, all-in self-pay estimate for your exact order, not an online average.

Before comparing prices, find out:

- the body part being scanned;
- whether the order is without contrast, with contrast, or with and without contrast;
- the CPT code the facility expects to bill; and
- what the quote includes.

## Why can the same MRI price vary by more than 30x?

The same MRI code can have very different prices because healthcare prices are set through separate hospital, insurer, and cash-payment systems. A higher price alone does not establish a better result.

| What changes the price? | Why it matters | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| **The billing code** | A brain MRI without contrast is different from one with and without contrast. | "What CPT code do you expect to bill?" |
| **The facility** | Hospital outpatient departments and independent imaging centers may set very different prices. | "Can my order be sent to another qualified facility?" |
| **The price type** | The gross charge, discounted cash price, and insurance-negotiated price are different numbers. | "What is the self-pay or discounted cash price?" |
| **What is included** | The radiologist, contrast, sedation, or facility charge may be billed separately. | "Is this the complete expected price for my order?" |

Hospitals must publish several types of standard charges. [CMS defines the **discounted cash price**](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/hospital-price-transparency-frequently-asked-questions.pdf) as the charge for someone paying cash or a cash equivalent.

That is not the same as the hospital's gross or [chargemaster price](https://unblindhealth.com/blog/what-is-a-chargemaster). If you simply ask, "How much is an MRI?" you may be given the wrong number for your situation.

## What do real patients report paying?

Patient stories are examples, not reliable averages. In a recent [Reddit discussion about MRI prices in Dallas](https://www.reddit.com/r/Dallas/comments/1rg1wvt/where_to_get_an_mri_for_a_reasonable_price/), one commenter reported about $400 self-pay versus more than $700 through a high-deductible plan. Another found a roughly $500 option but said the center could not perform the specific scan needed.


## How to find a lower MRI cash price

The best way to compare MRI prices is to use the same order and billing code at every facility, then request the complete estimate in writing.

### 1. Get a copy of the MRI order

Get the exact scan description, then ask the facility which CPT code it expects to bill. Ask the ordering clinician before changing the facility or any part of the order.

### 2. Call more than one facility

When clinically appropriate, compare a hospital outpatient department with independent imaging centers.

Use the same script each time:

> "I am paying without insurance. What is the complete self-pay price for this order and CPT code? Does it include the facility charge, contrast if ordered, and the radiologist's interpretation?"

If something is excluded, ask who will bill it and request that estimate.

### 3. Ask for a written Good Faith Estimate

If you are uninsured or not using insurance, providers generally must give you a Good Faith Estimate when requested or when care is scheduled at least three business days ahead. [CMS explains this protection](https://www.cms.gov/medical-bill-rights/know-your-rights/no-insurance). An estimate may cover only one provider, so you may need another from the radiology group.

If a provider's bill is at least $400 more than its estimate, you may qualify for the federal dispute process. Keep the estimate and compare it with every bill you receive.

### 4. Compare the complete price, not the first number

Compare the same services and watch for:

- a separate radiologist bill;
- contrast or medication charges;
- sedation, if needed;
- an added hospital or facility charge; and
- a deposit that is not the final price.

### 5. Verify that the facility is appropriate

Ask whether the location can safely perform the scan, especially if you need sedation, have an implanted device, or require specific equipment. You can also check the [American College of Radiology's accredited facility search](https://www.acr.org/Accreditation/Accredited-Facility-Search).

## Should you ask for the cash price if you have insurance?

Yes. Compare the cash price with your estimated cost through insurance. A cash payment may not count toward your deductible or out-of-pocket maximum, so ask your insurer before deciding.

This is the same tradeoff that can occur with prescriptions: [paying cash may lower the immediate price while changing how the purchase is treated by your plan](https://unblindhealth.com/blog/pay-cash-for-prescription).

## FAQ

### Is an MRI cheaper at an independent imaging center?

It can be. Prior insurer data cited in a [2025 peer-reviewed study](https://academic.oup.com/healthaffairsscholar/article/3/5/qxaf092/8122480) showed hospital outpatient imaging prices two to three times physician-office prices. That concerns negotiated prices, not a guaranteed cash-price difference, so compare local estimates.

### Can I take my MRI order to another facility?

Possibly. Ask whether the order can be sent elsewhere and whether the facility is appropriate. If using insurance, confirm network status and prior authorization before switching.

### Is a cheaper MRI lower quality?

Price alone does not establish quality. Confirm the facility can perform the exact scan and safely meet your needs.

### Is the radiologist's fee included in the MRI price?

Not always. Ask whether the quote includes the scan and professional interpretation. If the radiologist bills separately, request that estimate.

## The bottom line

The 36x MRI price spread is a warning not to accept the first number you see. For a scheduled scan, get the exact order and CPT code, compare qualified facilities, and ask each one for a written, all-in self-pay estimate.

Do not delay medically necessary imaging because of a price question. If cost is a barrier, tell the ordering office and ask about clinically appropriate lower-cost locations.

> **Price information, not medical advice:** This is not medical, legal, insurance, or individualized financial advice. Confirm the medical plan with your clinician and the current price directly with the facility.
