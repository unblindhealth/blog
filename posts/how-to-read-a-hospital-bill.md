A hospital bill can run several pages and list dozens of charges you've never
heard of. The good news: once you know the structure, it's far less intimidating
than it looks.

## Start with the itemized statement

The first bill you receive is often a **summary** — one or two lines with a big
number. Always request the **itemized statement**, which breaks the total into
individual charges. Hospitals are required to provide it on request.

Each line typically includes:

- A **CPT or HCPCS code** — a standardized code for the procedure or service
- A short **description**
- A **quantity** and a **unit charge**
- The **line total**

## What the numbers actually mean

The charge on your bill is usually the hospital's **gross charge** — the
list price. It's almost never what you (or your insurer) actually pay.

| Term | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| Gross charge | The hospital's full list price |
| Negotiated rate | What your insurer agreed to pay |
| Cash price | The discounted price for self-pay patients |
| Allowed amount | The maximum your plan will count toward the service |

> If you're uninsured, ask for the **cash price** — it's frequently a fraction
> of the gross charge.

## Cross-check every line

Billing errors are common. Compare the itemized statement against:

1. Your **Explanation of Benefits (EOB)** from your insurer
2. What you remember actually receiving during your stay
3. The hospital's published price for that CPT code

That last step is exactly what UnblindHealth is for — look up the code and compare it
across payers and hospitals before you pay.

## If something looks wrong

Call the billing department and ask for an itemized review. Duplicate charges,
services you never received, and incorrect quantities are the most common
mistakes — and they're worth disputing.
