**Short-term health insurance is temporary medical coverage designed to fill a limited gap between longer-term health plans.** It is not the same as an ACA Marketplace plan and can have different exclusions, underwriting rules, benefits, and consumer protections.

The key word is **temporary**.

## Who typically uses short-term health insurance?

**It is most relevant when you have a known, relatively short gap and have already compared comprehensive coverage.**

Examples include:

- your old job coverage ends and your new employer plan starts in one or two months
- you are between jobs and need temporary coverage while deciding what comes next
- you do not qualify for Marketplace savings and are comparing the cost of temporary coverage with full-price comprehensive insurance
- you missed another enrollment opportunity and short-term coverage is available in your state

It is generally a bridge product, not a long-term replacement for comprehensive insurance.

## Why can short-term insurance cost less?

**A lower premium can come with fewer protections or more exclusions.** Short-term, limited-duration insurance is generally outside many of the federal ACA rules that apply to comprehensive individual-market coverage.

Depending on the policy and state, that can affect:

- pre-existing-condition coverage
- prescription drugs
- maternity care
- mental-health services
- benefit caps
- medical underwriting
- renewal or reapplication

That is why you should not compare a short-term premium with a Marketplace premium as if the two products are identical.

## Does short-term insurance cover pre-existing conditions?

**Do not assume it does.** The answer depends on the policy and applicable state rules.

Before buying, ask in writing:

1. Is any condition I already have excluded or limited?
2. Are my current prescriptions covered?
3. Is there a dollar cap on benefits?
4. Are hospital, emergency, specialist, mental-health, and maternity services covered?
5. What happens if treatment began before the policy start date?

If you cannot get clear answers, the low premium is not enough information to make a useful comparison.

## How long can short-term insurance last in 2026?

**There is no single duration you should assume applies everywhere in 2026.** Check both your state's rules and the exact policy being offered.

A 2024 federal rule narrowed short-term plans to an initial term of no more than three months and a maximum duration of four months including renewals or extensions.

But in 2025, federal agencies said they would not prioritize enforcement of that definition while reconsidering the rule. So if you are shopping in 2026, look at the policy's actual start and end dates, whether it can be renewed, and any state-specific limits before enrolling.

## Does the No Surprises Act protect short-term-plan members?

**Not in the same way it protects people with comprehensive group or individual health insurance.** CMS identifies short-term, limited-duration coverage among the exceptions to the federal No Surprises Act billing protections.

That is another reason to know exactly what kind of coverage you are buying.

## When does short-term coverage make the most sense?

**The clearest use case is a short, known gap when you understand the exclusions and can tolerate the financial risk.**

Suppose your current coverage ends September 30 and your new employer plan begins December 1. Short-term coverage may be one option to explore for October and November. But before buying it, check whether you can instead use a Marketplace Special Enrollment Period or another employer plan. Those options provide comprehensive coverage and may be subsidized.

For the timing, see [Special Enrollment After Losing Health Insurance](https://unblindhealth.com/blog/special-enrollment-after-losing-health-insurance).

## When is short-term insurance a poor fit?

**It can be a poor fit when you expect to rely on the plan for ongoing or expensive care.** That is especially true if you have regular prescriptions, active treatment, planned maternity care, or a pre-existing condition that the policy may exclude or limit.

The question is not only “What is the monthly premium?” It is also **“What happens financially if I actually need care?”**

## Bottom line

Short-term health insurance can be useful as **temporary bridge coverage**, but it is not simply a cheaper Marketplace plan.

Before buying, confirm the policy duration, exclusions, benefit limits, prescription coverage, hospital coverage, and your state's rules. Then compare it with any comprehensive coverage you can access through a Special Enrollment Period.
