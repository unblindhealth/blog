Every hospital maintains a giant internal price list called the
**chargemaster** (formally, the *charge description master*, or CDM). It's the
starting point for almost every dollar figure on your bill — and one of the most
misunderstood documents in American healthcare.

## What's in it

A chargemaster is a database with tens of thousands of rows. Each row maps a
service, supply, or procedure to a **gross charge** — the list price. That
includes everything from a complex surgery down to a single dose of medication
or a box of gauze.

Each entry generally has:

- An internal item number
- A **CPT/HCPCS code** where applicable
- A description
- The gross charge

## Why the prices look so high

Chargemaster prices are notoriously inflated and inconsistent between hospitals.
A procedure listed at \$5,000 at one hospital might be \$18,000 at another a few
miles away. Several factors drive this:

1. **Historical inertia** — prices get marked up year over year from an old base
2. **Negotiating leverage** — a high list price is a starting point for insurer
   negotiations
3. **Cost shifting** — covering the gap left by lower government reimbursements

## Who actually pays chargemaster prices?

Almost no one, in full:

- **Insured patients** pay their plan's **negotiated rate**, often far lower.
- **Medicare and Medicaid** pay fixed, regulated amounts unrelated to the list price.
- **Uninsured patients** are the most exposed — but can usually request a
  **cash/self-pay discount**.

## Why transparency matters

Since 2021, U.S. hospitals have been required to publish their standard charges,
including gross charges, cash prices, and payer-negotiated rates. That data is
what makes tools like UnblindHealth possible: you can finally compare the real price
of a procedure across hospitals instead of guessing from an inflated list price.
